    Instructions

    Making a listening exam that cannot be paused or rewound

    A step-by-step guide. Allow about ten minutes the first time, and two minutes after that.

    What this does

    You give it an audio file and a few settings. It gives you back a web link. When a student opens that link and presses Start, the recording plays from beginning to end. They cannot pause it, rewind it, skip ahead or speed it up, and they only get the number of plays you allow — even if they reload the page.

    You paste that link into DigiExam. Every exam you make gets its own link. You do not need a new tool or a new account each time.

    Before you start

    • Your recording saved as an MP3. Most programs do this with File → Export → MP3. A WAV will work but is many times larger and slower to load.
    • Your DigiExam exam, ready to add a question to.
    You do not need any new accounts or logins. Recordings go into a shared Google Drive folder and are copied across automatically. Step 1 shows how.

    Step 1 — Put your recording in the shared Drive folder

    Skip this if your recording is already in the list — go straight to Step 2.

    1. Save your recording as an MP3. In most programs that is File → Export → MP3. Mono at 96–128 kbps is plenty for speech.
    2. Give it a name you will recognise in a list, such as french-l2-track1.mp3.
    3. Open the shared recordings folder in Google Drive. The link is in the tool, under How do I add a new recording? on Step 2.
    4. Drag your MP3 into that folder. That is the whole job.
    5. Wait about five minutes, then go back to the tool and press Refresh the list.

    Recordings are copied out of that folder automatically every few minutes. You do not need an account for anything, and nobody has to do it for you.

    Do not use a Drive share link in the tool. Google blocks other websites from playing files straight out of Drive, whatever the sharing settings say. Dropping the file in the shared folder is what makes it work — the tool plays its own copy.

    Step 2 — Build the link

    1. Open the Exam Audio Player page (the address your IT team gave you — bookmark it).
    2. Name the recording. Type what students should see at the top, such as Level 2 French — Listening, Track 1. Press Next.
    3. Click your recording in the list. The page checks it and tells you how long it is. If it is not there yet, do Step 1 above first.
    4. Set the rules. Choose how many times students may hear it, how much quiet reading time they get first, and how long the gap between plays should be. Press Create my link.

    Step 3 — Test it yourself

    1. Press Try it out first. This opens the page exactly as students will see it, but nothing you do counts against the play limit.
    2. Press Start and listen right through. Check the sound is clear and loud enough — there is no volume control beyond the device's own.
    3. Check the reading time and gap feel right. If not, close the tab, press Back and change something, and create the link again.

    Step 4 — Put it into DigiExam

    1. Press Copy link.
    2. Open your exam in DigiExam and go to the listening question.
    3. Paste the link into the question so students can click it.
    4. Add a sentence telling students what to expect, for example:
      Click the link and press Start when you are ready. The recording cannot be paused, rewound or replayed beyond the plays allowed.
    5. The one-off bit: DigiExam only lets students open approved websites. The first time anyone at school uses this, ask IT or your exams administrator to allow the player's address. Because the recordings sit on that same address, that is the only one needed. Once done it covers every exam, so you never have to ask again.

    Step 5 — On the day

    • Tell students beforehand that it cannot be paused, and to have pen and paper ready.
    • Have students test their headphones before the exam starts.
    • Keep a backup: a copy of the audio you can play over a speaker if a device misbehaves.

    If something goes wrong

    "Google Drive will not let other websites play its files." This is not your sharing settings, and changing them will not help — Google blocks it for every Drive file. Put the MP3 in the audio folder instead, as described in Step 1.

    Your recording is not in the list. Recordings are collected from the Drive folder every few minutes, so give it five and press Refresh the list. If it still has not appeared: check it is actually an audio file (only .mp3, .m4a, .wav and similar are picked up), and that you put it in the shared folder rather than your own My Drive. After ten minutes, tell whoever looks after the tool.

    "This recording is still being published." Normal for a minute or two after a recording first appears. Press Check again.

    A student sees "Link problem". The link was cut short when it was pasted — these links are long. Copy it again and re-paste the whole thing.

    A student says they have no plays left before starting. Someone else used the same device for the same exam within the last six hours. Clear the browser's site data on that device, or move the student to another one.

    A student's audio stopped partway. Almost always their network. The page loads the whole recording before enabling Start where it can, so this is rare — but this is what your speaker backup is for.

    You need to change an exam you have already built. Open the tool, press Editing an existing link? Paste it here on the first screen, paste your old link, and all the settings come back. Change what you need, create a new link, and swap it in DigiExam.
